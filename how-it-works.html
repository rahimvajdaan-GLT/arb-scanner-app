How sports arbitrage works

Sports arbitrage occurs when the best available prices across bookmakers make it possible, in theory, to cover every mutually exclusive outcome and receive more back than the total amount staked.

The core formula

For a two-way market with decimal odds A and B, calculate 1/A + 1/B. If the result is below 1, the quoted prices form a mathematical arbitrage before considering execution issues. A three-way market uses the same idea across home, draw and away.

How the stake split is calculated

Each outcome receives a share of the total stake proportional to its inverse odds. This aims to make the payout from every covered outcome approximately equal. Kaka Arb rounds the recommended stakes and uses the lowest resulting payout when estimating the effective return.

Why a mathematical arb can still fail in practice

Odds may change before both wagers are accepted. A bookmaker may reduce or reject a stake. Two operators may grade apparently similar markets differently. Fees, commissions, void rules and palpable-error policies can also change the result.

Moneyline, spreads and totals

Kaka Arb can compare match-winner or moneyline markets as well as supported spread and totals opportunities. A valid comparison requires equivalent events, selections, lines and settlement rules.

Best practice before acting

  1. Open both bookmaker markets and confirm they refer to the same event and market.
  2. Confirm the quoted prices are still available.
  3. Check maximum stake and account limits.
  4. Review void, overtime and settlement rules.
  5. Enter the exact accepted stake amounts and re-check the minimum payout.
Kaka Arb is an analysis tool, not a guarantee of profit. The scanner should be treated as a starting point for verification.
